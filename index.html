01 · Abstract

A connected view of the field.

Target detection is not only a model-design problem. It is shaped by the data available, the assumptions built into a detector, and the constraints of the application.

Remote sensing target detection is a key capability for interpreting Earth observation imagery, but it presents challenges that differ from conventional natural-image detection: objects may be tiny, densely distributed, arbitrarily oriented, or embedded in complex backgrounds. Variations in imaging platforms, sensors, spatial resolution, and geographic context further complicate model development and deployment. This review brings together three closely related dimensions—data, models, and applications—to provide a structured view of the field. It considers how datasets, annotation practices, and evaluation protocols influence research; organizes major detection approaches and the design choices used to improve localization, multi-scale representation, robustness, and efficiency; and relates technical progress to practical application requirements. The review also highlights open challenges and opportunities for building more reliable, generalizable, and deployable remote sensing detection systems.

03 · Data

Detection starts with the data.

Remote sensing datasets differ in their acquisition platforms, sensing modalities, ground sampling distance, scene coverage, and labeling conventions. Understanding these differences is essential when comparing methods or judging whether a model will transfer to a new setting.

Acquisition: satellite, airborne, and UAV imagery with different spatial and temporal coverage.
Modalities: optical, SAR, and other sensor data with complementary properties.
Benchmarking: annotations, train/test splits, class balance, and metrics must be interpreted in context.

05 · Applications

Methods matter in context.

Application requirements determine which trade-offs matter most—from broad-area monitoring to fine-grained recognition and timely response.

Urban & infrastructure

Mapping buildings, roads, facilities, and built environments over large areas.

Agriculture & environment

Supporting resource inventory, environmental observation, and land-use analysis.

Transportation & maritime

Detecting vehicles, ships, and other objects in dense or wide-area scenes.

Disaster response

Helping locate relevant objects and infrastructure changes under time pressure.

06 · Open challenges

What still needs to be solved?

Strong benchmark performance does not automatically guarantee robust performance across sensors, regions, seasons, and deployment platforms. The next step is to connect algorithmic progress with reliable evaluation and real operational needs.

01

Small, dense, and oriented targets

Preserving fine details and precise localization when targets occupy only a few pixels or appear at arbitrary angles.

02

Generalization across domains

Maintaining performance across geography, sensors, resolution, acquisition conditions, and unseen target distributions.

03

Data efficiency and fair evaluation

Reducing annotation burden while making comparisons reproducible, representative, and robust to dataset bias.

04

Deployment at scale

Balancing accuracy, latency, memory, energy, and interpretability in operational sensing systems.
